Welcome {name}

Thank you for participating in our study!

In this study, you will learn about two topics:

  1. Whether Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) has already been achieved.
  2. How Large Language Models (LLMs) may affect human cognition and learning.

For each topic, you will complete the following activities.

1. Read

You will first read a short passage introducing key ideas and arguments related to the topic.

2. Recall

After reading, you will be asked to explain what you learned in your own words and to the best of your ability.

3. Discuss

You will then have three rounds of discussion with our AI learning partner. During the discussion, you can explain your ideas, consider different perspectives, and respond to questions or arguments.

4. Search

After each discussion round, you can interact with our conversational search agent to explore anything you would like to learn more about or verify.

For example, you may use it to clarify a concept, check a claim, find supporting evidence, or explore a question that came up during the discussion.

5. Answer Questions

After all three discussion rounds, you will answer several questions about the topic. These questions will help us assess your understanding of what you have learned.

6. Create Concept Maps

Before and after the discussion, you will also create a concept map with causal relationships.

A concept map shows the important concepts in your understanding of a topic and how they are connected. In this study, we are especially interested in causal links—whether you believe one concept influences another.

  • Each node represents a concept.
  • Each arrow represents a causal relationship.
  • The direction of the arrow shows which concept influences the other.

For example, if you believe that greater reliance on AI reduces mental effort, and reduced mental effort affects learning, you might draw:

Greater AI Reliance → Reduced Mental Effort → Changes in Learning

There is no single correct map. Please include the concepts and relationships that best represent your own understanding at that moment.

By comparing your maps before and after the discussion, we hope to understand how your knowledge and understanding of the relationships between concepts may change during the study.
