# Topic: AI Extends Human Cognition
# Phase: 1
# Learning Goal
How does AI redistribute limited human cognitive resources through cognitive offloading?

# Reading Material
Human cognition is powerful but limited. Working memory can maintain only a restricted amount of information at once, and complex tasks can overwhelm these limited resources (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974; Sweller, 1988). Cognitive offloading provides a solution: humans routinely use external resources to reduce internal demands, from writing notes to using calculators and digital devices (Risko & Gilbert, 2016). AI dramatically expands what such external resources can do.
From the extended mind perspective, cognition does not have to occur entirely inside the brain. When an external resource becomes reliably integrated into thinking and problem solving, it can function as part of a larger cognitive system (Clark & Chalmers, 1998). AI can now retrieve information, organize complex material, summarize alternatives, and maintain information that would otherwise compete for limited mental resources. Rather than simply doing less thinking, users can redistribute their cognitive effort toward tasks requiring judgment, interpretation, and decision making.
Research on external memory supports this possibility. Saving information externally can improve memory for subsequently encountered information because cognitive resources no longer need to maintain everything internally (Storm & Stone, 2015). Research on digital technologies similarly suggests that technology often changes what people remember rather than causing a general decline in memory (Pearson, 2025).
AI therefore need not be viewed as a substitute for human cognition. By strategically moving some cognitive operations outside the brain, AI can extend the effective capacity of human cognition and allow limited cognitive resources to be used where they matter most.
